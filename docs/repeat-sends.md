# Repeat-send escalation (#124)

Laya scored a Russian "can anyone sell USDT at a reasonable rate?" ask at 0.6407 and kept it. The same chat received a message with the same length and the identical score about 3.5 hours later, and it was kept too. Prompt and example edits could not lift that ask without breaking controls (see #124), so Nikita chose a bot-side rule instead: when one account sends the same content again, in the same group or in another group the bot moderates, each send raises the score, and once the score crosses the gate the account's earlier copies are deleted as well.

## Rule

- **Same content.** The text or caption is decomposed with Unicode NFKD and lowercased. Invisible format characters and combining marks are dropped, a small set of Cyrillic and Greek lookalike letters fold to Latin, and anything that isn't a letter or digit becomes one space. Matching is exact on that normalized form. There is no fuzzy near-duplicate matching, which keeps the rule conservative.
- **Same account.** The key is `sha256(actor + "\0" + normalized text)`, where the actor is the existing namespaced real actor (`user:<id>` or `chat:<sender_chat.id>`). Different accounts sending the same text never add up.
- **Eligible copies.** These are new or edited text/caption messages that reached a completed classification (or a cache verdict) with a model score of at least **0.5** (`REPEAT_MIN_SCORE`) and at least **30** normalized characters (`REPEAT_MIN_CHARS`). Uncaptioned media, failed classifications, skipped updates, short texts ("+", "спасибо", "11", "ок, понял") and anything the model scores under 0.5 are never recorded or boosted. A single send behaves exactly as before.
- **Boost.** `effective = 1 − (1 − p) × 0.5^n`, where `p` is the current copy's model score and `n` is the number of the account's other copies whose send times fall within **24 hours** (`REPEAT_WINDOW_MS`) of this one, in any group. Each other copy halves the distance to 1, and `n` is capped at 20.
  - A second copy is deleted when `p ≥ 0.60`. The #124 ask goes from 0.6407 to 0.8204.
  - A third copy is deleted when `p ≥ 0.50`.
  - Under 0.5 nothing ever changes.
- **Deletion.** When `effective ≥ SPAM_THRESHOLD` (0.80; the gate itself is unchanged), the current copy is deleted together with every earlier, not-yet-handled copy in the window, in every group, through the normal audit-reported, stats-counted deletion path (`rule: "repeat_send"`). When the model itself deletes a copy, the earlier copies in the window go too. A copy whose deletion was attempted, whether it succeeded or failed, is marked handled and never retried. Later copies within the window are deleted when they arrive.
- **Edits** keep the copy's original send time and handled state. An edit is not a new send. An edit that leaves the copy ineligible (shorter, scored under 0.5, or no longer moderated at all, such as a removed caption) removes it. Earlier copies in another group are skipped when the account has become exempt there (administrator, or that group's linked channel) or the check fails.

## Choices and data

These choices come from the content-free production logs for 2026-10-13 15:19 to 2026-10-14 14:39 UTC (container lifetime). The logs only record IDs, lengths, link counts and scores, so a cluster of the same length, link count and 4-decimal score is a *candidate* repeat. It can't distinguish senders, so the counts below are upper bounds.

- **The #124 ask itself.** Two 66-character copies in chat `-1003806664297` (msgs 34977 and 34979, 08:52 and 12:15 UTC), both scoring 0.6407 with no context. No same-shaped copy appears in other chats in the window. The logs hold no sender identity, so "same account" is likely but unproven. The second copy was 3 h 23 min after the first, which is why the window is 24 hours rather than the 10 minutes that message history uses. 24 hours is also the spam cache's TTL.
- **Same group vs other groups.** Both count equally. The observed miss repeated within one group, and a repeat from the same account is the same evidence wherever it lands. Weighting cross-group copies more would make an everyday ask cross-posted to two groups at a 0.5–0.6 score deletable at the second send.
- **Floor 0.5.** Kept messages fill 0.10–0.80, and the bulk of benign traffic sits at 0.20–0.50. Under the floor, repeats are left to the model.
- **Minimum 30 characters.** Repeated short texts are common across different people. In the log window, 52 of the 194 repeat clusters scoring 0.5–0.8 were under 24 characters. The 30-character floor still covers short asks such as "Кто купит usdt? По хорошему курсу".
- **What it would catch.** 328 kept 52-character messages scored ≥0.5, and 251 of them were followed within three message IDs by a model-deleted spam message, which points to a spammer preamble that currently survives. Replaying the rule on the clustered logs, up to 138 clusters and 707 messages (≥30 chars, scores 0.5–0.8) would have been deleted. Model deletions in the same window were 4,607.

## Storage, bounds and privacy

`repeat_sends` (migration `007`) stores only `chat_id`, `message_id`, `copy_key` (the actor+content hash), `sent_at` and `handled`. It holds no text and no sender ID. Rows older than twice the window are pruned every ten minutes, and the table is capped at 100,000 rows (oldest first). Each lookup returns at most 20 copies, unhandled ones first, so a long run of already-deleted copies cannot hide an older undeleted one. Every query is bounded to 500 ms with at most two in flight (marking a copy handled or removing an edited copy waits for a slot instead of being dropped), over connections kept warm for 15 minutes, and a slow or unavailable database means no escalation, never a deletion. If removing an edited copy fails, the process remembers it (up to 10,000 entries), never returns it as an earlier copy, and retries the removal on the next prune. Because the rows live in PostgreSQL, the history survives restarts and deploys.

Audit snapshots of copies this process saw stay in memory (2,000 entries) so each deleted copy is reported with its own content. A copy recorded before a restart (or evicted by that cap) has no snapshot. Its report uses the current copy's body, which is the same content after normalization, and its context notes the substitution.

Logs add `repeat_send_detected` (chat/message IDs, copy count, other-chat copy count, model and effective score, decision) and `repeat_store` health counters. `spam_deleted` gains `otherChatCount` and, for a repeat-only delete, `effectiveProbability`. As before, nothing logs text or sender identity.
